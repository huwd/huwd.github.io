I am a bit mystified by this book.

I picked it up based on its blurb.
On the basis of which I expected to enjoy it more.
Taiwan, language, food, travel, a good character drama?
Ticks all my boxes.

At the same time, I've been kind of mystified as to why it appears to be so popular with others?
I keep seeing it in bookshops tagged with loving notes.
Or gushing book reviews.
It apparently did quite well on the award circuit.

I'm sure there's a story there but I've not looked into it.

What I found instead was a curious book, I did indeed love the travel, food and characters.
I found the dynamic between its two main characters fascinating,
and was as grateful as the protagonist to be taken on a tour of so much great food.

I expected it to go harder on Taiwan's post-colonial history.
But what was there seemed subtle and muted to me.
There's perhaps a meta discourse of this as a book pretending to be "found text" that I've missed.

I found the way the names of food were romanised a bit jarring, personally I would have preferred to consume the pinyin or characters directly.
So I missed something translating food items back into things I might have come across before.

Whilst all this was enjoyable, the pace felt very odd.
Like it was indecisive about its own narrative movement, pushing forward then hesitating.
Mix in, a real love of long lists and at times it felt less like a book, and more like something serialised in internet era media.
Like my RSS feed has picked up on some old tour of a writer, inexplicably surfaced on Buzzfeed.

I enjoyed it for what it is, and perhaps more for interests I already hold and brought to it.
That all feels quite niche though.
I would be curious to know what everyone else got out of it though?
